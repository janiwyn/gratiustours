Welcome to Uganda

Where the rainforest meets the savannah

Uganda packs an astonishing range of wild places into one country. Track mountain gorillas through misty Bwindi in the morning, watch elephants and lions on the open plains, cruise past hundreds of hippos, and stand where the Nile thunders through a narrow gorge at Murchison Falls.

At Gratius Tours we design private trips around you: your dates, your pace, your budget. We handle permits, lodges, transport and guides, so all you do is enjoy the wild.

Wildlife you'll meet

Uganda's Wild Residents

From gentle giants of the forest to the big cats of the plains, here's who you could meet on safari. Hover over a card to learn more.

Bwindi & Mgahinga

Mountain Gorilla

Gorilla beringei beringei

More than half of the world's remaining mountain gorillas live in Uganda. Spend one unforgettable hour with a habituated family.

Kibale Forest

Chimpanzee

Pan troglodytes

Our closest living relatives share about 98% of our DNA. Kibale is often called the primate capital of the world.

Ishasha & Murchison

African Lion

Panthera leo

In the Ishasha sector of Queen Elizabeth, lions are famous for lounging high up in fig trees, a rare sight in Africa.

Queen Elizabeth

Leopard

Panthera pardus

Elusive and solitary. Your best chance is an early-morning or late-evening game drive, when leopards are on the move.

Murchison & Queen Elizabeth

African Elephant

Loxodonta africana

The largest land animal on Earth. Herds often gather near water, especially along the Nile and the Kazinga Channel.

Kazinga Channel

Hippopotamus

Hippopotamus amphibius

The Kazinga Channel has one of the highest concentrations of hippos in Africa. See them up close on a boat cruise.

Ziwa Rhino Sanctuary

White Rhino

Ceratotherium simum

Ziwa is the only place in Uganda where you can track rhinos on foot, a great stop on the way to Murchison Falls.

Murchison & Kidepo

Rothschild's Giraffe

Giraffa camelopardalis

A rare giraffe subspecies. Look for its "white stockings": no markings below the knees.

Queen Elizabeth

African Buffalo

Syncerus caffer

Large herds wallow in the shallows of the Kazinga Channel to keep cool in the midday heat.

Queen Elizabeth & Murchison

Uganda Kob

Kobus kob thomasi

This graceful antelope appears on Uganda's national coat of arms, and you'll see plenty on the plains.

Wetlands nationwide

Grey Crowned Crane

Balearica regulorum

Uganda's national bird, proudly displayed in the centre of the national flag.

Lakes & channels

Great White Pelican

Pelecanus onocrotalus

One of over 1,000 bird species in Uganda, a true paradise for birdwatchers.

Bucket-list moment

A Day Tracking Mountain Gorillas

Wondering what gorilla trekking is really like? Here's how your day unfolds in Bwindi Impenetrable Forest.

Early morning
Briefing with the rangers

Meet the Uganda Wildlife Authority rangers at the park office. They'll tell you about the gorilla family you'll visit and the trekking rules.

The trek
Into the "impenetrable" forest

Follow trackers along muddy trails and through thick vegetation. Depending on where the family moved overnight, the hike can take from one to several hours.

The magic hour
Face to face with gorillas

Spend one precious hour watching the silverback, mothers and playful babies go about their day, just metres away.

Afternoon
Certificate & celebration

Walk back to receive your gorilla trekking certificate, then relax at your lodge with stories you'll tell for a lifetime.

Good to know
Permit requiredPermits are limited each day, so book early. We arrange them for you.
Minimum age 15Trekkers must be at least 15 years old.
Small groupsA maximum of 8 visitors per gorilla family each day.
Keep your distanceStay about 7 metres away to protect the gorillas' health.
Dress for the jungleHiking boots, long sleeves, a rain jacket and gardening gloves.

Planning Your Uganda Trip

Everything first-time visitors usually ask us, in one place.

When is the best time to visit?

Uganda sits on the equator, so it's warm all year. The dry seasons are the most popular, but every month has something to offer.

Local tip: Gorilla permits sell out fastest for June–September and December–February. If you have dates in mind, send us a request early and we'll secure your permits.

Most visitors need a visa, which you can apply for online before you travel. If you're also visiting Kenya or Rwanda, ask about the East Africa Tourist Visa. A yellow fever vaccination certificate is required on arrival.

International flights land at Entebbe International Airport, on the shores of Lake Victoria. We'll meet you at arrivals and take care of all transfers from there.

  • Sturdy, waterproof hiking boots
  • Light rain jacket and a warm layer for cool mornings
  • Neutral-coloured, long-sleeved clothing
  • Binoculars and a camera with extra batteries
  • Insect repellent, sunscreen and a hat
  • Gardening gloves for gripping vegetation on treks

Speak with your doctor about vaccinations and malaria prevention before you travel. Drink bottled or treated water. Our guides are experienced, carry first-aid kits and keep you safe around wildlife at all times.

The local currency is the Uganda Shilling (UGX). Cash is useful in rural areas and markets, while larger hotels and lodges usually accept cards. Tipping guides and porters is customary and always appreciated.

English is an official language and widely spoken, along with Swahili and many local languages such as Luganda. Ugandans are famously friendly. A simple "Oli otya?" (How are you?) always earns a smile!
